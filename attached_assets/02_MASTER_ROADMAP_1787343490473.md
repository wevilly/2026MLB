# Master Product Roadmap

## Goal

Build a persistent MLB research operating system that understands current MLB state, player skill, pitcher shape, bullpen availability, park context, external bettor reasoning, historical prediction performance, and market-specific mechanisms, then produces auditable daily research and eventually calibrated predictions for four independent hitter markets.

## Roadmap overview

### Gate A - Phase 2A Closeout
Formalize current Player/Pitcher/Park research acceptance. No new product scope unless a blocking data defect must be fixed.

### Phase 2B - Bullpen Research and Availability Foundation
Turn the existing bullpen ledger philosophy into persistent database state and operational UI. No market predictions yet.

### Phase 3 - Market Research Engines
Build four separate non-probabilistic research/ranking engines for TB, XBH, Walk, and HR. Engines expose mechanisms, evidence, counter-evidence, opportunity, and data-quality flags. No FIRE probabilities yet.

### Phase 4 - Historical Settlement and Feature Store
Build a clean historical dataset pairing pregame feature snapshots with official outcomes. Add process-error taxonomy and immutable settlement.

### Phase 5 - Model Validation and Calibration
Train and evaluate separate models for TB, XBH, Walk, and HR using walk-forward validation. No model becomes active because it looks good on one split.

### Phase 6 - Daily Ranking, Confidence, and Board
Activate calibrated model outputs and research-confidence statuses only after Phase 5 acceptance. Build daily market boards and game-level research summaries.

### Phase 7 - Bettor Intelligence
Ingest external bettor/capper picks, reasoning, timing, mechanisms, duplication lineage, and settlement. Measure who adds signal by market/mechanism. No popularity voting.

### Phase 8 - AI Analyst
Give the AI read-only access to trusted internal state plus live web research and uploaded evidence. The AI challenges, explains, compares, and researches. It cannot rewrite frozen predictions, official outcomes, snapshots, or active model versions.

### Phase 9 - Daily Operations, Automation, and Workbook Export
Automate refresh sequencing, health checks, slate generation, ledger updates, settlement, postmortems, and compatibility exports including `slate.json` and the existing workbook builder.

### Phase 10 - Production Hardening and UX Finalization
Performance, caching, observability, permissions, disaster recovery, mobile/responsive polish, user-facing audit trails, and final documentation.

## Dependency rule

No downstream phase may silently compensate for an upstream data defect.

Examples:
- Market engines cannot guess bullpen availability.
- Models cannot backfill missing handedness with zeros.
- AI cannot infer official lineups.
- Settlement cannot use FantasyPros outcomes when MLB official event data exists.

## Parallelization rule

Safe to parallelize:
- independent UI work against frozen contracts;
- test fixtures and regression suites;
- source-documentation research;
- historical source imports that do not mutate current state;
- independent market feature exploration after the shared feature store is frozen.

Do not parallelize when tasks compete to define:
- canonical identity;
- settlement rules;
- bullpen availability truth;
- model target labels;
- confidence semantics;
- current source precedence.

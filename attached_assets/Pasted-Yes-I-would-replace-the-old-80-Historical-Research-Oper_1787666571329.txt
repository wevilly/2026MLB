The original plan had the right ambition around season history, handedness, day/night, pitch-type context, market separation, lineage, and postmortems, but it still treated some external sources too much like live APIs and did not emphasize strongly enough that **the platform itself must build and retain a baseball understanding of every player**. 

# #80 Revised - MLB Player Intelligence & Historical Research System

## What & Why

Build a permanent **MLB Player Intelligence System** for the existing MLB Analyst Platform.

The objective is not merely to store statistics and not merely to research today's slate.

The objective is for the platform to develop a continuously updated, source-traceable understanding of every relevant MLB hitter, starting pitcher, and bullpen arm so that today's matchup analysis begins with historical knowledge already stored in the platform.

The system should eventually be able to answer questions such as:

* How does this hitter perform against right-handed pitching?
* How does he perform against left-handed pitching?
* Is that split supported by enough plate appearances?
* How does he perform in night games?
* Does his apparent day/night split remain after accounting for park and opponent quality?
* How does he hit sliders?
* How does he hit 95+ mph four-seam fastballs?
* How does he perform against sweepers with large horizontal movement?
* Has his contact profile changed over the last 14, 30, or 60 days?
* Is his recent HR spike supported by barrels and exit velocity?
* How does this pitcher perform against left-handed batters?
* What has changed in this pitcher's arsenal during the season?
* How does he perform the first, second, and third time through the batting order?
* Which relievers are actually likely to face this portion of the lineup tonight?
* What previous pitchers most resemble today's opposing pitcher?
* Does the hitter's historical performance against that pitcher archetype support today's market?
* Is Batter-vs-Pitcher history actually representative of today's version of the pitcher?
* What evidence supports a 2+ TB, XBH, Walk, or HR conclusion?
* What evidence argues against it?
* Has this exact type of research thesis worked historically?

The system must distinguish:

**raw facts**
from
**derived statistics**
from
**analyst interpretation**.

The website should not depend on FantasyPros projections to tell it whom to pick.

FantasyPros should primarily tell the platform **who is expected to play today**.

The MLB Analyst Platform should decide **who it likes and why**.

---

# Core operating philosophy

The system should follow this principle:

> **FantasyPros identifies today's player universe. Historical baseball data teaches the platform who those players are. Independent matchup research determines the picks.**

FantasyPros projections may remain stored as outside reference information.

They must not be treated as the website's own baseball opinion.

The system should eventually be able to compare:

**FantasyPros view**

versus

**MLB Analyst independent research view**

and learn from disagreements.

# What success looks like

A successful implementation means every relevant MLB player has a persistent profile that becomes more complete over time.

When today's projected lineup arrives, the site should not begin learning that hitter from scratch.

It should already know his:

* season history
* career context where available
* handedness splits
* day/night history
* home/road history
* pitch-type performance
* velocity-band performance
* batted-ball profile
* plate-discipline profile
* recent trends
* historical BvP
* historical matchup archetypes
* lineup-role history
* park interaction
* injury/role changes
* settled market history where applicable

Today's research then asks:

> **How does everything we already know about this hitter interact with everything we already know about today's pitcher, bullpen, park, weather, and batting-order position?**

# 2. Three layers of player knowledge

Every player profile should explicitly distinguish three levels.

## Layer 1 - SOURCE FACT

An observation from a source.

Example:

* 421 PA
* 18.4% K rate
* .472 xSLG
* 43.1% hard-hit rate

Required metadata:

* source
* observation date
* season
* sample size
* denominator
* retrieval timestamp
* effective date

Classification:

**VERIFIED**

when directly checked and normalized from the named source.

---

## Layer 2 - DERIVED FACT

A calculation performed by MLB Analyst from stored observations.

Example:

> K% is 3.1 percentage points below his career baseline.

Or:

> His 30-day barrel rate is higher than his season rate.

Or:

> Slider usage has risen from 21% season-long to 34% over his last five starts.

Every derived value must be reproducible.

Classification:

**SOURCED**

or an equivalent explicit derived-data state.

---

## Layer 3 - ANALYST INTERPRETATION

What the measurements mean.

Example:

> The hitter is producing more contact without losing quality of contact, which strengthens his two-hit route to 2+ TB.

Or:

> His recent HR increase appears unsupported because HR/FB has risen while barrels and 90th percentile EV remain flat.

Classification:

**INFERENCE**

Never present interpretation as measurement.

# 48. Postmortem learning

Do not classify every losing pick as bad research.

Each outcome should be reviewed for:

### Data failure

Input was wrong/stale.

### Opportunity failure

Player lost PA or role.

### Matchup failure

Research mechanism did not occur.

### Model/research failure

Evidence weighting was wrong.

### Variance

Thesis occurred but result did not.

Also preserve:

**GOOD PROCESS / LOSS**

and

**BAD PROCESS / WIN**

when appropriate.

# Definition of Done

This phase is not done because a large dataset exists.

It is done when:

1. Every relevant player has one canonical identity.
2. Historical observations are source-traceable.
3. The platform can produce season and rolling profiles.
4. Hitter RHP/LHP splits work.
5. Pitcher LHB/RHB splits work.
6. Day/night profiles work with sample caveats.
7. Home/road profiles work.
8. Pitch-type profiles work.
9. Pitcher arsenal histories work.
10. BvP profiles are generated internally.
11. BvP includes sample/representativeness treatment.
12. Bullpen arms have individual profiles.
13. Today's FantasyPros projected lineup can immediately retrieve existing player intelligence.
14. Daily research does not rebuild historical data.
15. Every market uses its own evidence mechanism.
16. Every pick can explain why it was selected.
17. Every pick can explain why it could fail.
18. Every source claim can be traced.
19. Every settled result remains attached to what the system knew pregame.
20. Future postmortems can measure which research ideas actually help.
